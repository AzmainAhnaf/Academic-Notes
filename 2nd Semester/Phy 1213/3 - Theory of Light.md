# Wave Theory of Light

Wave Velocity $\ne$ Phase Velocity


**Particle Velocity:** Light is a kind of disturbance that propagates through a hypothetical medium called ether according to Newton.

**Transverse Wave:** The wave moves in the direction perpendicular to the direction of the oscillation of the particles.


## Huygens's Principle

Every point on a primary wave front act as a secondary source of disturbance.
![[Pasted image 20250725224617.png]]

These secondary wave or wavelets spread out from each of these secondary source with the same speed as primary wave.

The surface that touches the secondary wavelets at any point gives the new wavefront.

### Limitations

![[Pasted image 20250725224920.png]]

According to Huygens's theory, there will be secondary wave front both in backward and forward direction.

It was based on the existence of the medium ether but Michelson-Morley experiment shows that there is not existences of such medium.

The solution comes with the theory of Stokes

## Theory of Stokes

The intensity = $1 + \cos{\theta}$
For, forward direction, $I = 1 + \cos{0 \degree} = 2$ 
For, backward direction, $I = 1 + \cos{180} = 0$
So there will be no backward secondary wave front.


# Differential Equation of Wave Motion

$$
\frac{d^2y}{dt^2} = v^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}
$$
From Maxwell's equation,
$$
\triangledown ^2\cdot E = \mu_o \epsilon_o \frac{d^2E}{dt^2}
$$
$$
\triangledown ^2\cdot E = \mu_o \epsilon_o \frac{d^2B}{dt^2}
$$
Now,
$$
\frac{d^2y}{dt^2} = v^2 \triangledown^2y
$$
$$
\Rightarrow \frac{1}{v^2} \frac{d^2y}{dt^2} = \triangledown ^2 y
$$
$$
\therefore \frac 1{v^2} = \mu_o\epsilon_o
$$
$$
v = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_o\mu_o}} = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}
$$
