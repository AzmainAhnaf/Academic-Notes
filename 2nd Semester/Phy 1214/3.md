
**What types of waves are produced in a vibrating tuning fork?**
The stationary waves which are transverse in nature are produced in a tuning fork

**How do we hear a sound from a tuning fork**
The vibrating prongs of a tuning fork produce longitudinal waves in air and the observer can hear that sound.

**Of what material a tuning fork is made?**
The forks are made of an alloy of steel, nickel, and chromium called elinvar. This is used because of its high elasticity.

**Can we alter the nature frequency of a tuning fork?**
Yes the frequency of a tuning fork can be decreased by increasing the load. Paste some mass with it like any cream or wax and it will be decreased

**Does temperature affects the frequency of a tuning fork?**
As the temperature increases, the length of the prongs increases and its elasticity decreases. As a result, the frequency of the fork decreases.

**In Melde's Experiment, what kind of waves are produced in the string?**
The wave in the string is transverse stationary wave, irrespective of the transverse or longitudinal mode.

**What is the difference between transverse and longitudinal modes?**
In transverse mode, the tuning fork is vibrating perpendicular to the string while in longitudinal mode the prongs of a tuning fork vibrate along the direction of the string.

**Why does sparking takes place at the point of contact of spring and the prong?**
The prong vibrates and the circuit breaks. Then a high e.m.f. is induced in the coil which builds up a large potential difference at the point of contact. Charges discharge from the pointed tip of the spring causing spark.

**When do the loops appear in this experiment?**
When the length of the thread between the pully and the prong becomes equal to the half of the wavelength of the waves travelling in it.

**On what factors the length of the loop depends?**
Basically, it depends upon the tension in the string and the mass per unit length of the string. for a fixed string it could be change by changing tension in the string

**What is the relation between the number of loops in longitudinal and transverse modes for a given tension?**
The number of modes in longitudinal mode is half the number of loops observed in the transverse mode.

**What is the natural frequency of a tuning fork?**
It is the frequency of the unforced vibration of the tuning fork when the damping is low. In other words it is the frequency at which a tuning fork can naturally oscillate after an initial excitation.
